# RoleVow — Manifesto

**The first Simple Tabletop RPG Tool, with the Negative Price Ultra-Guarantee.**

**Play your role, now it's your roll. Our vow? To make you say wow.**

Adapted from the [Divertoscopio manifesto](https://github.com/massimilianobanini/divertoscopio/blob/main/MANIFESTO.md). This is the English project manifesto, not a performance certification.

## Why we exist

A brilliant ruleset, a beautiful map, a prepared Game Master or sophisticated AI can all fail to create an enjoyable session. The result is not the quantity of material produced. Did the people playing have fun, and want to return? RoleVow aims to reduce the distance between wanting to play and actually playing. Time to the first meaningful choice and time to the first enjoyable moment are distinct. We want less unnecessary work, not speed at the expense of fun.

## 1. RPGs exist for people, not for material.

The result matters more than the number of pages produced: it is the experience of the people playing.

Different groups enjoy different things. A memorable NPC for one table may be forgotten by another; an adventure that works for one group may not work for the next. Judge the experience for these people in this context.

## 2. Fun is not assumed.

Observe, listen and check what worked, then improve what did not.

Chance belongs in the game, not in our understanding of its quality. Silence, long preparation and polished AI responses are not proof of enjoyment. Ask, observe and use the evidence to improve.

## 3. Personalize before standardizing.

Different tables and people have different needs. The method must adapt.

Learn people's preferences, personalize, play, observe, understand problems, adapt and test again. A good GM already does this; RoleVow aims to make the process less laborious, not replace human sensitivity.

## 4. Different ways to play. The same final question.

In person, online or in chat: did people have fun?

A physical table, live online play and chat each have different social, material and scheduling tradeoffs. None is inherently superior. Current hosted play is one human relay host in one chat; this is not a synchronized multi-account room.

## 5. AI is a means, not the goal.

It should remove practical obstacles, not replace people's creativity.

If you already enjoy playing without AI, keep doing so. Use AI only when it removes an obstacle or adds value. Its sophistication is irrelevant if people are bored.

## 6. Depth when you want it. Simplicity at the start.

Those who want more detail can explore it. Those who want to play now need not study first.

Make depth available without making it obligatory. Users need not become engineers or memorize technical terminology before receiving value.

## 7. Less work that does not create fun.

Especially for GMs: less wasted preparation, more value at the table.

Preparation, maps and rules can be fun. Reduce only unwanted, repetitive or unused work. A GM retains final authority and chooses what to delegate.

## 8. Real freedom, real consequences.

Players' choices must be able to change the world, including when they make mistakes.

The player owns voluntary character choices. Clarify risks and options without deciding what a character wants or thinks. Mistakes and defeat must remain possible.

## 9. Failure is information.

An unsuccessful experience provides an opportunity to correct and test again.

Real use → feedback → understand the issue → change → retest. Each version should learn from unsuccessful experiences, rather than hiding them.

## 10. The risk should not be yours alone.

Within its terms, the Ultra-Guarantee gives a real cost to an unmet promise.

RoleVow costs €0. Eligible dissatisfied adults who truly use it may request a symbolic €1 net reputational indemnity under the [published terms](guarantee/TERMS.md), subject to verification, route feasibility and funding. The principle is real economic accountability for an unmet promise, not buying feedback. The risk should not fall entirely on the user.

## 11. Open does not mean finished.

The project states its limitations and must improve through real use and testing.

Publication is the start of learning. Accumulate real tests, successes, failures, revisions, stated limitations and evidence. Do not claim that untested features are proven.

## What we do not want to become

A replacement for every human GM, a machine that produces campaigns for their own sake, an encyclopedia users must study, a system that decides what everyone enjoys, or a design intended to keep people playing indefinitely. Voluntary return is more important than engagement for its own sake.

## Our north star

**Maximize people's real enjoyment and their voluntary desire to play again.** AI, rules, measurements, documentation and procedures are tools, not results.

Try RoleVow. Tell us what worked and what did not. Help the next version become more useful and more enjoyable.
