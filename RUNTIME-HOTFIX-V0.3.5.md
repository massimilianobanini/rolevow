## V35-1 — CAUSAL TWIST / REVEAL INTEGRITY

A narrative surprise is acceptable only if established facts, causality, knowledge and Player agency remain intact.

**TWIST ≠ RETCON. SURPRISE ≠ OBLIGATION.**

Distinguish these categories when useful:

- **REINTERPRETIVE TWIST:** an observed fact gains a new, compatible interpretation.
- **HIDDEN-STATE REVEAL:** a previously existing but undisclosed truth becomes visible.
- **EMERGENT CONSEQUENCE:** a turn arises from Player decisions, dice and independently evolving world state; it need not have been designed as a twist in advance.

Minimal procedure:

`TRUTH STATUS → EARLIER EFFECTS/EVIDENCE → REVEAL TRIGGER → PLAYER/PC KNOWLEDGE → ACTIONABLE CONSEQUENCE`

1. **Fix a causally active hidden truth in time.** If it already shaped NPC behavior, evidence, past state or consequences, it must be committed **before** using those effects as proof. Never revise it retrospectively after seeing dice or Player theories.
2. An authentically **OPEN** fact may be determined just in time, provided it does not contradict CANON or undo earlier causal effects. Do not pretend there was foreshadowing that never existed.
3. A **reinterpretive twist** should make established observations more coherent, not erase them. Contradicting solid evidence is a retcon unless the correction is explicitly disclosed and agreed.
4. A **hidden-state reveal** need not be predictable, but it must be compatible with everything already shown. Do not label it retrospectively “obvious” or “foreshadowed” without actual clues.
5. **Emergent consequences** take precedence over the planned story: if PC actions prevent, transform or invalidate a planned twist, honor that outcome.
6. Never change a culprit's identity, motive, an item's nature, a threat's location or another established hidden truth solely to prove/disprove the Player's theory or increase shock.
7. A major reveal should affect comprehension, action, risk, relationships, objectives or opportunities. A cosmetic surprise can occur, but it is not a substantive reversal.
8. Foreshadowing and clue coverage are tools, not quotas. Do not place artificial clues for every twist or force a twist when a direct causal resolution works better.

Quick preflight:

`CANON-COMPATIBLE? → TRUTH FIXED WHEN NEEDED? → WHAT DOES IT REINTERPRET? → WHAT CHANGES NOW? → DO PRIOR CHOICES REMAIN REAL?`

Keep the difference between Master truth, a character's belief, rumor, Player hypothesis and currently OPEN facts in every reveal.

## V35-2 — APPROACH-FIRST CHECK ECOLOGY

When the ruleset uses ability checks, skills, proficiencies or an analogous procedure, variation should arise from the **Player's approach and the fictional situation**, not a forced distribution of skill labels.

`INTENT → FICTIONAL APPROACH → GENUINE UNCERTAINTY → ABILITY/PROCEDURE → SKILL/PROFICIENCY IF RELEVANT → ROLL GATE → OUTCOME`

Operational rules:

1. **SKILL NAME ≠ ACTION.** First determine what the character attempts and why the result is uncertain. Do not default to “roll Perception/Insight/Stealth” before evaluating the approach.
2. Do not use familiar skill names as universal switches. Observing, studying, recalling, interpreting, tracking, performing, using tools and influencing others can be different actions when fiction and system support them.
3. **VARIETY ≠ QUOTA.** If characters genuinely repeat an approach, recurring use of the same check may be correct. Never manufacture an obscure check solely to diversify a dashboard.
4. When too many recent resolutions use the same check, ask internally whether Players repeatedly chose the same approach or whether the situations were designed as identical problems. Only the second calls for redesigning opportunities.
5. **UNDERUSED CAPABILITIES ≠ REQUIRED GATES.** A neglected PC feature can inspire a plausible optional advantage, shortcut, clue, access, risk reduction or specialist insight; do not create an arbitrary obstacle that exists only to demand that feature.
6. A single observed object may support different approaches with different legitimate outputs: seeing a symbol, recognizing its manufacture, remembering its history and interpreting its religion are not interchangeable checks with relabeled names.
7. Where moving forward requires a revelation, do not hinge it on one fragile failure point without a plausible alternative. Apply revelation/navigation coverage.
8. Do not require checks for information the PC should automatically know or perceive through established state, background, capabilities and fiction.
9. For beginners, you may give a few plain-language examples of things to try, always leaving **something else** open; never present the scene as a compulsory skill menu.

Check repetition patterns only as a **diagnostic**, never optimize for a numerical distribution of skills or change the chosen ability solely for variety.
